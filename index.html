Before

Every booking was a conversation

An aircon cleaning, repair and installation shop in Bacolod City. The shop ran on the owner's personal number and a Facebook page.

The booking

A call or a Facebook message. The address, the landmark and "which aircon?" asked one question at a time.

The schedule

In the owner's head and his chat history. The technicians were told who, where and when by message.

"Is it still on?"

The customer rang again to ask. The owner scrolled back to find the answer.

The month

No count of bookings, no idea how fast anyone was rung back, no list of whose aircon is due a check.

The rule I build by: anything that makes a person ask, retype or remember by hand is worth removing. Every screen in this deck removes one of those.
SpeakerThere was no system to replace — there was a phone, a chat and a good memory. And no customer list to import: the app starts empty on purpose.
After

Every message became a screen that does the work

A chat holds words. An app holds the job: who it is for, what is on it, whose turn it is, and when it was done.

The customer

A booking that arrives complete

The page asks everything the owner used to ask on the phone — once, in order, with nothing left out.

  • A cart of aircons. Tap Window, Split, Cassette… each one gets its own services, brand and model
  • Prices as they tap, when the owner switches them on
  • The landmark, pushed hard — in Bacolod it is how a technician finds the gate. Pin the house, or pick it on a map
  • Only open days. Sundays and the owner's closed dates cannot be picked
  • A reference code back at once, like WA-7241
  • One plain privacy sentence and a tick box before it sends (Data Privacy Act)
A failed booking never looks like a successful one: the page says so, and keeps everything typed.

The office

New bookings, one tap to a job

The Inbox is the office's morning: every new booking with a red count, what they want, the day they asked for and what it comes to.

  • Call and Map on every booking — the landmark and the pin come with it
  • Accept makes the customer file and the job, already on the day they asked for
  • A call the owner takes goes in as "By phone", so every job lives in one place
  • Reject needs no reason typed; the customer's page says so
  • A booking from an existing customer is accepted onto their file — never a second copy of the same person

Paperwork

His quotation, already filled in

The owner writes quotations today, in Word. The app makes his layout — letterhead, subject, the table, the note, his signature and the customer's Conforme line — from the booking.

Quotation

  • Lines and note editable, saved on the booking
  • Send as PDF to Messenger, Viber or email
  • Accept takes its total as the job's price

Receipt

  • A4, from the job, for the technician to hand over
  • GCash number printed on it
  • The office marks it Paid — cash, GCash or other
No PDF library: the phone's own print screen makes the file. Nothing extra to download or break.

The office

The schedule, in one place

Today, tomorrow, upcoming, no date yet, done. Morning or afternoon — not a promised minute a shop cannot keep.

  • Route — today's stops, morning first, opened in Google Maps

Repeat business

Every customer's aircons, and when they are due

A customer file is made when a booking is accepted — never straight from the public page. It keeps their aircons, their jobs and their bookings.

  • A regular check per customer: every 1, 2, 3, 6 or 12 months from a chosen date
  • Check-ups is the owner's call list. He rings; Book it makes the job
  • A week after the job: "is it working well?" — All good, or book a visit
  • Customers on a map, and Book again from any past job
  • Delete a customer for real — every phone forgets them (the privacy law's right to be removed)
No SMS and no automatic reminders: the owner calls. A message nobody reads is cost, not value.

What it is for

Three numbers, nobody counting

The app was built to answer three questions from its own records. A screen that moves none of them was not built.

1
Bookings through the page, per week

Against the calls and messages it replaces. Phone bookings are kept out, so the number stays honest.

2
How fast the office rings back

From the booking to the office's first action. Measured first, judged later.

3
Done on the day promised

The job keeps the promised day and when it was really done, or this could never be answered.

Plus a month's takings by cash, GCash and other, exported in one tap.

No developer needed

The owner changes it himself

Everything a shop changes month to month is a screen in Admin, not a phone call to me.

  • Services & prices — one price per service per appliance; a new service shows on the page at once
  • Appliances — aircons, freezers, washing machines; his own picture for each; hide one without losing its history
  • Two switches: show prices on the booking page; show prices to technicians
  • Page photos, closed days, the shop's GCash, his quotation and receipt
  • Users: anyone signs up; the owner makes them a technician or an admin, or switches them off

Security

A public page, and nothing public behind it

The booking page's key to the database is public — anybody can read it from the page. So the whole design assumes a stranger holds it.

A stranger with the key

Can do three things

Send a booking · look up a booking they have the code and number for · read the service list

→
The database

Three functions, no tables

No read of any table. Not customers, not jobs, not the team — not one row.

→
Staff

Signed in, by role

What an account may see is a database rule, checked on every request.

Proven, not promised

A test runs as the stranger and tries to read every table. Every read must be refused — it runs after every change to the database.

Public input is untrusted

A booking never becomes a customer on its own. A person in the office accepts it first. A hidden trap field catches bots; one booking a minute per number.

Privacy law

Name, number and address are personal data (RA 10173): one plain sentence before sending, and the owner can remove a customer completely.

SpeakerThis is the one rule whose breach could not be undone — the key is public the moment the page ships. So it was designed first and tested before anything else was built.

How I work

I decide what it is for, then build only that

The three numbers came first. Everything else had to move one of them, or wait.

1
Ask the owner

Eleven questions before a line of code. One phone call settles more than a week of guessing.

2
Safety first

The public key's limits were built and tested before the page existed.

3
Test the real thing

On a phone width and a PC width, with made-up data — never a real customer's.

4
Ship with notes

Every version says what changed, in words the owner uses.

What I chose not to build is part of the work. Invoices, parts and stock, SMS, push notifications, email updates, customer ratings and photos from customers were all considered — and parked, because each would open to an empty screen on day one. A screen nobody opens is cost, not value.
